# Twitter thread (draft)

**Thread goal:** Use arXiv:2603.05472 as a hook to explain why “3σ evidence for evolving dark energy” can be fragile once you account for model complexity (Bayesian evidence) and dataset tension (SN calibration).

## Tweet 1
DESI DR2 headlines: “evolving dark energy at ~3σ.”
A new Bayesian reanalysis (arXiv:2603.05472) asks: does that survive *Bayesian evidence* + *tension checks*?

## Tweet 2
Key idea: adding parameters (like w0–wa) almost always improves best-fit χ².
Bayesian evidence asks whether the improvement is worth the extra flexibility (Occam penalty).

## Tweet 3
Result (from abstract): DESI DR2 BAO + Planck → ln B = −0.57 ± 0.26.
That *modestly favors ΛCDM*, despite the σ-level frequentist headline.

## Tweet 4
When supernovae enter, calibration matters.
With corrected DES-Dovekie calibration: ln B ≈ −0.01 ± 0.27 (no real preference).

## Tweet 5
With the *original* DES-SN5YR calibration: ln B = +3.32 ± 0.27 (strong extension preference).
And the paper traces that to a calibration error that creates internal ΛCDM tension.

## Tweet 6
Takeaway: in 2026 cosmology, systematics can masquerade as new physics.
Evidence ≠ Δχ² alone.
Tension diagnostics are part of the physics.

## Tweet 7
Why this matters: if dark energy truly evolves, it’s a fundamental shift in our picture of the vacuum / gravity / the fate of the Universe.
But the case must be *robust across datasets and calibrations*.
