# Paper Metadata

**Title:** Neutrino mass limits and decaying dark matter: background evolution versus perturbations

**Authors:** Thomas Montandon, Vivian Poulin, Thomas Rink

**ArXiv ID:** 2603.03284

**Category:** Cosmology (astro-ph.CO)

**Date:** 2026-03-03

**Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03284

## Abstract

We revisit cosmological neutrino mass bounds when a fraction of dark matter is allowed to decay to massless dark radiation. By compensating the late-time increase in the matter density induced by neutrinos becoming non-relativistic, decaying dark matter (DDM) can render datasets solely sensitive to the background density effectively insensitive to neutrino masses. Using data from baryonic acoustic oscillations (BAO) and Type Ia supernovae together with a distance prior from the cosmic microwave background (CMB), we find that the one-sigma upper limit on the total neutrino mass changes from ≈0.11 eV in the standard ΛCDM case to ≈0.4 eV when allowing for DDM. We provide full numerical results and analytical approximations for the effective equation of state of DDM, the evolution of the dark matter fraction, and the modification to the growth of perturbations.

## Key Concepts

- **Neutrino Mass:** The rest mass of neutrinos; constrained by cosmological observations
- **Dark Matter Decay:** The hypothesis that dark matter is unstable and converts to dark radiation over cosmic time
- **Dark Radiation:** Relativistic particles (like neutrinos or axions) that act like radiation in the early universe
- **Background Density Evolution:** How the universe's matter density changes over cosmic history
- **BAO (Baryon Acoustic Oscillations):** Imprints of sound waves in the early universe, preserved in galaxy clustering
- **Perturbations:** Small density fluctuations that grew into galaxies and galaxy clusters

## Why It Matters

This paper reveals a hidden degeneracy in cosmological data: if dark matter decays, current observations become insensitive to neutrino masses. This suggests we may need independent measurements (non-cosmological) to properly constrain neutrino masses if dark matter is indeed unstable.
