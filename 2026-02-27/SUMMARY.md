## **Option 1: Cosmology & Magnetic Fields**

### Imprints of primordial magnetic fields on the late-time Universe
- **arXiv:** 2602.23263 (astro-ph.CO)
- **Authors:** Jennifer Schober, et al.
- **Focus:** High-resolution MHD simulations of primordial magnetic field evolution through structure formation
- **Key Finding:** Dynamo amplification competes with gravitational compression; survival of primordial signatures depends on spatial scales
- **Impact:** Informs observational searches for early-universe magnetism signatures

**Summary:** Investigates whether magnetic fields seeded in the early Universe leave detectable imprints on today's cosmic structure. Using direct numerical simulations with varying viscosity (Reynolds number regimes), the authors find that turbulence-driven dynamo amplification can significantly modify magnetic energy spectra. The critical insight: primordial magnetic field memory persists only below the Jeans scale, with the competition between dynamo growth and free-fall time determining the final state. Essential for interpreting current and future structure formation observations.

## **Option 2: Particle Physics & Dark Matter**

### Ruling Out Spiky WIMP Dark Matter using Indirect Searches
- **arXiv:** 2602.23348 (hep-ph)
- **Authors:** Dibya Chattopadhyay, et al.
- **Focus:** Constraints on dark matter density spike at the Galactic Center using existing indirect detection data
- **Key Finding:** Fermi-LAT and MAGIC observations rule out DM spikes for WIMP masses 10 GeV – 100 TeV
- **Impact:** Tests theoretical predictions; narrows WIMP parameter space

**Summary:** The supermassive black hole Sgr A* at our Galaxy's center should gravitationally "compress" dark matter into a density spike—if the spike exists, it would boost annihilation signals. This paper leverages existing gamma-ray (Fermi-LAT, MAGIC) and neutrino (IceCube) data to constrain the spike profile across a wide WIMP mass range. A milestone result: even if photons represent only 1% of the annihilation products, the gamma-ray constraints remain binding. Near-term experiments could further test this.

## **Option 3: Quantum Information & Sensing**

### Butterfly Echo Protocol for Axis-Agnostic Heisenberg-Limited Metrology
- **arXiv:** 2602.23332 (quant-ph)
- **Authors:** Gregory Bentsen, et al.
- **Focus:** A new quantum sensing protocol that uses chaotic dynamics for rotation measurement
- **Key Finding:** Achieves Heisenberg-limited (quantum-optimal) sensitivity without complex state preparation
- **Impact:** Practical path to next-generation quantum sensors; near-term experimental feasibility

**Summary:** Quantum metrology aims to extract maximum information from quantum systems. Standard approaches require specially engineered "anticoherent" quantum states—difficult and expensive to prepare. This paper shows that chaotic circuits (easy to prepare with random one-axis twists) can serve the same role. The "butterfly echo" protocol then maps small rotations into measurable signals. Dephasing requirements are stringent but achievable in near-term platforms (e.g., lanthanide atoms), opening a practical route to quantum-enhanced magnetometry and other sensing applications.
